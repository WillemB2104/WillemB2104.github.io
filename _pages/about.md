I'm a postdoctoral researcher in **precision psychiatry**, holding positions at
[Amsterdam UMC](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/en/persons/willem-bruin/) and
[Leiden University](https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/en/staffmembers/willem-bruin).

My research sits where neuroimaging, machine learning and clinical psychiatry
meet. The broad question I keep returning to is a simple one that turns out to
be very hard: **can we measure something about an individual brain that
meaningfully informs how that person will do, and what treatment will help?**

## What I work on now

At Amsterdam UMC I work in the Team Neuropsychiatry group at the VUmc, in
collaboration with **Prof. dr. Odile van den Heuvel**, on how the brain
develops from childhood into young adulthood and how deviations from typical
trajectories relate to obsessive-compulsive symptomatology.

At Leiden University I'm based at the Institute of Education & Child Studies,
within Forensic Family & Youth Care.

## Background

I completed my **PhD in 2024** at Amsterdam UMC, Department of Psychiatry, on
biomarker development for psychiatry: diagnostic markers and treatment-response
predictors built from machine learning and multimodal neuroimaging data.

[Read the thesis](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/142745422/Development-and-validation-of-a-multimodal-neuroimaging-biomarker-for-electroconvulsive-therapy-outcome-in-depression.pdf){: .btn .btn--primary}

## Consortium work

Much of what I do happens at a scale no single site can reach. I've led and
coordinated analyses within international consortia including
[**ENIGMA**](https://enigma.ini.usc.edu/) (and
[ENIGMA-Anxiety](https://enigma.ini.usc.edu/ongoing/enigma-anxiety/)) and
[**GEMRIC**](https://mmiv.no/gemric/), working across 70+ sites for
data-driven biomarker development and validation.

Studies I led within these consortia:

- **The functional connectome in obsessive-compulsive disorder: resting-state
  mega-analysis and machine learning classification for the ENIGMA-OCD
  consortium.** *Molecular Psychiatry* (2023).
  [doi:10.1038/s41380-023-02077-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-023-02077-0)

- **Development and validation of a multimodal neuroimaging biomarker for
  electroconvulsive therapy outcome in depression: a multicenter machine
  learning analysis.** *Psychological Medicine* (2023).
  [doi:10.1017/S0033291723002040](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291723002040)

- **Structural neuroimaging biomarkers for obsessive-compulsive disorder in the
  ENIGMA-OCD consortium: medication matters.** *Translational Psychiatry*
  (2020).
  [doi:10.1038/s41398-020-01013-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-020-01013-y)

This kind of work is unglamorous and essential: harmonising protocols,
reconciling heterogeneous data, and building analyses that hold up across
populations rather than fitting the quirks of one scanner in one hospital.

## Approach

I care about methods that are honest about uncertainty. A prediction model that
performs beautifully in a held-out split of the same dataset and collapses at a
new site hasn't told us much. The work I find worth doing is the kind that
survives contact with a new sample.
